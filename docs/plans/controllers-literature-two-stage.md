# Literature search: flat (A) vs two-stage (B) decision structure

Terms: *goods-based choice* = choosing among the things on offer, independent of the movement needed to get them; *action-based choice* = the competition is among movement plans themselves; *option* (Sutton et al.) = a temporally extended action with its own policy and termination; *model-free/habit* = a cached value learned from outcomes; *model-based/planning* = value computed from a model of consequences. Tags: [full] = full text read, [abs] = abstract or secondary summary, [rec] = recalled, not re-checked.

## 1. What the evidence says, by source

**Goods vs actions (neuroscience).**
- Padoa-Schioppa & Assad 2006 [abs], Padoa-Schioppa 2011 [full, [PMC3273993](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3273993/)]: OFC neurons encode offer/chosen value with "less than 5% of OFC neurons significantly modulated by the spatial configuration of the offers"; choice is made in goods space and "the choice outcome then guides a good-to-action transformation", which the author admits "is poorly understood". Cai & Padoa-Schioppa 2014 [full, [PMC3951647](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3951647/)] dissociated offers from saccade targets in time and space: OFC/LPFC encoded the chosen good *before* targets appeared, then LPFCv→LPFCd converted it into an action plan; a "hybrid" (start in goods space, finish in action space) was tested and rejected. Wunderlich, Rangel & O'Doherty 2010 [abs]: vmPFC chosen-value signal appears before the stimulus–action pairing is revealed, in humans. Established: when actions are *not* known at offer time, the brain chooses the good first, then maps to action (a two-stage structure). Interpretation: these are binary, two-good tasks; nothing here speaks to 10–30 candidates.
- Cisek 2007 [full, [PMC2440773](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2440773/)], Cisek & Kalaska 2010 [rec]: "instead of viewing the functional architecture of behaviour as serial stages of representation, we view it as a set of competing sensorimotor loops"; multiple reachable targets are parallel peaks in one population, biased by expected reward (basal ganglia a "promising candidate" for the bias). Cisek concedes that "decisions based on abstract rules may first be expressed in frontal regions and propagate backward". This is (A) in spirit: one competition among concrete, currently available action opportunities, with no separate goal stage. Cisek 2012 [abs] softens to "decisions emerge through a distributed consensus across many levels of representation".
- Hunt & Hayden 2017 [full, first ~60%, [PMC5621622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5621622/)]: "comparison is not the unique purview of any single brain area"; the serial sequence evaluate→compare→select is "an emergent property of the system"; Box 1 says decision spaces "may reflect the anatomical connections of a given region, or even be artefacts of the experimental design". Hierarchy exists but is temporal/abstraction (anterior PFC tracks block-wise changes, posterior trial-by-trial), not "goal stage then action stage". Yoo & Hayden 2018 [title only; abstract not retrievable]; Hayden & Moreno-Bote 2018 [abs]: choice is serial accept/reject of the attended option, the neuron's referent set by attention.
- Kolling et al. 2012 [abs]: humans switch between a "foraging" mode (engage with what is here vs search elsewhere; dACC) and a "comparison" mode (which of these options; vmPFC); later work disputes the clean split [abs].

**Foraging and ethology.** Charnov 1976, Stephens & Krebs 1986 [rec]: patch-leaving and prey accept/reject are separate decisions, each against a long-run rate threshold; normative, not mechanistic. Hayden 2018 [abs]: "the elemental decision in foraging is the accept-reject decision and not ... a binary choice between simultaneously presented items". Tinbergen 1951, Baerends 1976 [rec/abs]: appetitive behaviour (seeking) is hierarchically above consummatory acts, which are released only when the stimulus situation (contact) is reached. This is (B) in its classical form and matches the contact constraint in the creature.

**Animat action selection.** Tyrrell 1993 [abs, [thesis](https://era.ed.ac.uk/handle/1842/20257)] compared mechanisms in a simulated animal and argued *against* hierarchies that decide at every level (information is lost at the top) and *for* free-flow hierarchies where activation flows down and the decision is made only at the bottom, i.e. a hierarchically informed flat decision. Bryson 2000 [abs] argues the reverse in the same environment (hierarchy plus sequence beats full parallelism). Maes 1990 [rec]: flat spreading-activation net. Redgrave, Prescott & Gurney 1999 [abs]: basal ganglia as a central selector among competing channels; Humphries & Prescott 2010 [rec]: ventral striatum selects at the level of strategies/goals, dorsal at the level of actions. The animat literature is divided on exactly our question.

**Computational hierarchy and arbitration.** Options (Sutton, Precup & Singh 1999) [rec]: a policy over options *and* primitive actions is flat at the top; hierarchy is inside the option. Botvinick, Niv & Barto 2009 [abs], Ribas-Fernandes et al. 2011 [abs]: neural support (pseudo-reward prediction errors for subgoals). Daw, Niv & Dayan 2005 [rec] and Lee, Shimojo & O'Doherty 2014 [full, [PMC3968946](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3968946/)] arbitrate at the *action* level: the weight P_MB mixes model-based and model-free values of left/right choices; goals are context, not choices. Dezfouli & Balleine 2013 [full, [PMC3854489](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3854489/)]: "the goal-directed system selects a goal, and then determines which habit should be executed to reach that goal"; habits are open-loop sequences; hierarchical family favoured at 99%. Cushman & Morris 2015 [full, [PMC4653221](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4653221/)]: the reverse. Model-free TD learns a value per goal option V(s,O), a greedy model-based policy picks the action most likely to reach it; four experiments, e.g. 85% vs 69% choice of the setup goal after reward vs punishment, t(216) = −11.1; fitted w_MFG = 0.74, w_MB = 0.00. Their rationale: habit fits goals that are "reliably valuable", planning fits the variable means; savings are largest "where the set of possible actions from any given state is very large". Ballard et al. 2024 [abs] found the facilitation but not the expected inflexibility, a partial challenge.

## 2. Does the answer depend on conditions?

Yes, and the dependence is the clearest result. Goods-first appears when the action is unknown at offer time (Cai & Padoa-Schioppa; Wunderlich). Action-space competition appears when offers are reach/saccade targets already in view (Cisek; Pastor-Bernier & Cisek 2011 [rec]) — and even then OFC still codes goods. Hunt & Hayden: the space depends on the task. Our creature: objects are spatially separate at different distances, and consummatory actions (eat, sleep-near) exist only at contact, so for far objects the only concrete actions are approach/avoid/observe — approach being a multi-step option. That matches ethology's appetitive→consummatory split and Padoa-Schioppa's "good first, action later" condition. For objects at contact the situation is Cisek's: a few concrete affordances, best resolved by direct competition. No study covers 10–30 simultaneous candidates; Hayden's serial attention + accept/reject is the closest natural-choice model.

## 3. Which controller owns which stage

Cushman & Morris (habit picks goal, planner picks action) and Dezfouli & Balleine (planner picks goal, habit executes) both fit two-stage human data. The reconciling principle, stated by Cushman & Morris, is: cache what is stable, compute what varies. In a static world, object values are stable, so a cached goal value (Cushman & Morris) is right; in the spec's phased world (devaluation/reversal, section 4) the object's value is what changes, so goal selection must be owned by the component that adapts — episodic memory or world model — while the fast operant handles the at-contact action. That is Mapa's assignment, and it is the Dezfouli & Balleine direction, so the contradiction with Cushman & Morris is one of *which quantity is stable*, not of principle. Note also that Cushman & Morris's "habit over goals" is TD over options; an episodic return cached per approached object (which the APPROACH-zeroing defect in section 3.3 currently destroys) is the same quantity, so Mapa's memory-selects-object is closer to their model than the naming suggests. Daw 2005/Lee 2014 give the better-supported mechanism than hard-wiring: run uncertainty-weighted arbitration *at each stage*, let both controllers report where they have evidence, and let ownership emerge from calibration. The spec's "operant does not value approach" is then an abstention at stage 1, not a hard-wired role.

## 4. Consistency with hierarchical JEPA (LeCun)

LeCun 2022 [full, local PDF, sections 3.2, 4.6, 4.7]. H-JEPA levels are *time scales/abstraction*: "JEPA-2 takes the representations extracted by JEPA-1 as inputs and extracts higher-level representations with which longer-term predictions can be performed." Hierarchical planning (Fig. 16): the top level minimises one high-level cost C(s2[4]) by inferring abstract actions that "are not real actions but targets for the lower level predicted states"; lower-level cost modules C(s[2]) "take a lower-level state and a high-level condition and measure to what extent the state satisfies the condition"; the lower level then finds actions minimising those subgoal costs. He notes "the intermediate vocabulary of actions" is usually predefined but should be learned, that the process "is top down and greedy" and joint optimisation across layers is better. Cost: C(s) = IC(s) + TC(s), the immutable intrinsic cost (hunger, pain) plus a trainable critic predicting future IC; the configurator weights submodules "to focus on different subgoals at different times". Only the top level carries drive cost; lower-level costs are derived. Later work: Director (Hafner et al. 2022) [abs] has a manager choosing latent goals at fixed intervals and a worker reaching them, on a fixed world model; a 2026 hierarchical planner ([arXiv 2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03208)) [abs] bolts hierarchical subgoal planning onto flat latent world models (V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM, PLDM) "without retraining them". V-JEPA 2-AC itself plans flat (CEM over action sequences) [rec].

Mapping to our design: (B)'s stage 1 ("which object") is exactly a LeCun level-2 abstract action — a condition "be at contact with object k" over a longer horizon — valued by the drive cost V(K|H); stage 2 is the level-1 problem. So (B) is structurally consistent with H-JEPA, with one difference: LeCun's lower level only satisfies the subgoal, whereas our stage 2 re-arbitrates value among actions on the object. Does flat conflict? Not fundamentally: the current `WorldModelFilter` (src/main/java/br/cefetmg/lsi/l2l/creature/actionSelector/WorldModelFilter.java, L114–140) scores each candidate with a one-step critic and returns the best, which is Mode-2 planning at horizon 1 on one level; both LeCun's framing and the 2026 bolt-on show hierarchy can live in the planner on top of a flat model. The practical conflict is bookkeeping: a hierarchical planner needs a per-object (goal) cost and per-object episodic returns, which a flat (object, action) ledger with APPROACH credit zeroed does not provide; and calibration (contract rule 4) must be checked separately for goal-level and contact-level candidates or a level mismatch hides inside one z-score.

## 5. Recommendation

The literature does not settle (A) vs (B); it is divided (Tyrrell vs Bryson; Cisek vs Padoa-Schioppa; Cushman & Morris vs Dezfouli & Balleine) and no study has our candidate count. What it does support: (i) the decision space follows task structure, and our structure (far objects, actions available only at contact, approach as a multi-step option) is the one where goods-first/appetitive-consummatory separation holds; (ii) arbitrate by uncertainty at whichever level choice occurs; (iii) a hierarchical planner can sit on top of a flat choice. I agree with the researcher that flat is easier and, once fixed (honest SE, abstention, one stop rule, APPROACH credit), is a sound Mode-2 horizon-1 scheme; note that flat is already "flat over options", since APPROACH(k) *is* the goal k. The staged path is defensible: keep flat now, but make it hierarchy-ready at zero cost — tag far-object candidates as goal-level and contact candidates as action-level in the decision record, keep episodic returns per object, calibrate the two kinds separately. If budget allows one arm, run (B) in the phased world (where the literature predicts the stages diverge: stable means, changing goal values); if not, defer (B) until the JEPA planner arrives, where LeCun's architecture makes it the natural form.
